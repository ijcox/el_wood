Fir Wood
Fir is a softwood widely used in construction, furniture, and woodworking, known for its straight grain, uniform texture, and ease of working.

Fir comes from trees of the genus Abies, native to North America, Europe, and Asia. Common varieties include Douglas Fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii) and Noble Fir (Abies procera).

Construction and Workability

  • Soft to medium-soft; easy to work with hand and power tools
  • Accepts nails, screws, and adhesives well
  • Finishes well with stains, paints, or varnishes
  • Popular in framing, millwork, and furniture components

Durability

  • Moderately durable; better for indoor than outdoor use unless treated
  • Resistant to splitting along the grain
  • Less decay-resistant than cedar or redwood

Pliability

  • Moderately flexible; can be bent with steam or careful shaping
  • Useful for curved architectural elements and furniture parts

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: light reddish-brown to pale brown
  • Sapwood: pale cream to white
  • Straight grain with uniform texture; occasional knots
  • Warm appearance suitable for both structural and decorative use

Cost

  • Low to moderate; widely available and affordable
  • Douglas Fir is highly prized for structural strength

Common Uses

  • Construction framing, beams, and plywood
  • Interior trim, moldings, and paneling
  • Furniture, cabinetry, and doors
  • Boat building and outdoor structures when treated
  • Flooring and joinery projects

Origin

  • Douglas Fir: Western North America
  • Noble Fir: Pacific Northwest, USA
  • Other Abies species: Europe and Asia

Other Characteristics

  • Strong, stable, and relatively lightweight
  • Good dimensional stability with minimal shrinkage
  • Straight grain makes it easy to mill and plane
  • Widely used for structural and decorative purposes
Fir is a versatile softwood valued for construction, woodworking, and furniture projects due to its strength, workability, and straight-grain appearance.
