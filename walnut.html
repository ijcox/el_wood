Walnut Wood
Walnut is a premium hardwood valued for its rich, dark color, fine grain, and excellent workability. It is widely used in high-end furniture, cabinetry, and decorative applications.

Walnut primarily comes from the genus Juglans, with black walnut (Juglans nigra) being the most common in North America, and European walnut (Juglans regia) cultivated worldwide.

Construction and Workability

  • Hard yet easy to work with hand and power tools
  • Excellent for carving, turning, and joinery
  • Finishes beautifully, accepting stains, oils, and varnishes
  • Stable and resists warping if properly dried

Durability

  • Very durable and resistant to decay
  • Suitable for furniture, flooring, and interior paneling
  • Not highly suited for prolonged outdoor exposure unless treated

Pliability

  • Moderate flexibility when steamed
  • Can be bent for curved furniture parts

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: rich chocolate brown to dark brown
  • Sapwood: pale yellowish-brown
  • Straight to wavy grain patterns; occasional curls and burls highly prized
  • Develops a deep, warm patina over time

Cost

  • High; black walnut is more expensive due to rarity
  • Highly figured wood (burls, crotches) significantly increases cost

Common Uses

  • High-end furniture and cabinetry
  • Gunstocks and instrument bodies
  • Veneers and paneling
  • Decorative turned items
  • Flooring and architectural millwork

Origin

  • Black walnut: Eastern United States
  • European walnut (English walnut): Southern Europe and Western Asia

Other Characteristics

  • Strong, dense, and impact-resistant
  • Excellent dimensional stability
  • Prized for aesthetic qualities such as color variation and figure
  • Less prone to splitting than some hardwoods
Walnut is highly prized by woodworkers and designers for its strength, stability, and luxurious appearance, making it ideal for fine furniture and decorative applications.
