Cherry Wood
Cherry is a fine hardwood prized for its smooth texture, warm reddish-brown color, and excellent workability.

Cherry wood comes from trees in the genus Prunus, native to North America and Europe. Black cherry (Prunus serotina) is especially valued in furniture and cabinetry.

Construction and Workability

  • Hard yet easy to work, sand, and carve
  • Accepts stains and finishes beautifully
  • Ideal for detailed cabinetry, fine furniture, and moldings
  • Resists warping and splitting when properly dried

Durability

  • Moderately durable; suitable for indoor use
  • Less resistant to outdoor weathering unless treated
  • Develops a rich patina over time, enhancing appearance

Pliability

  • Moderate flexibility when steamed
  • Can be bent for curved furniture components

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: light reddish-brown to deep reddish-brown
  • Sapwood: pale yellowish-white
  • Fine, straight grain with occasional wavy patterns
  • Develops a warm, darker tone with age and exposure to light

Cost

  • Moderate to high, depending on grade and availability
  • Highly figured cherry (curly or birdseye) is more expensive

Common Uses

  • Furniture and cabinetry
  • Musical instruments (pianos, guitars)
  • Interior trim and moldings
  • Wood veneers
  • Paneling and decorative accents

Origin

  • Black cherry: Eastern North America
  • European cherry (Prunus avium): Europe and Western Asia

Other Characteristics

  • Excellent sanding and finishing properties
  • Moderate strength and hardness
  • Develops a smooth, satiny texture
  • Less prone to splitting and checking than some hardwoods
Cherry wood is highly valued for fine furniture and cabinetry due to its workability, aesthetic appeal, and rich color that matures beautifully over time.
