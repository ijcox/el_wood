Acacia Wood
Acacia is a hardwood prized for its strength, durability, and rich golden to reddish-brown color. It is widely used in furniture, flooring, and decorative woodworking.

Acacia comes from trees of the genus Acacia, native to Australia, Africa, and Southeast Asia. Its dense grain, natural oils, and resistance to decay make it suitable for indoor and outdoor applications.

Construction and Workability

  • Hard, dense, and durable; moderate to difficult to work with hand and power tools
  • Accepts nails, screws, and adhesives well, though pre-drilling may be needed
  • Can be sanded and polished to a smooth, glossy finish
  • Used in fine furniture, cutting boards, and turned objects

Durability

  • Highly durable; resistant to rot, decay, and insect attack
  • Excellent for both indoor and outdoor use
  • Longevity makes it suitable for flooring and decking

Pliability

  • Moderate flexibility; can be bent using steam with care
  • Useful for curved furniture and specialty woodworking pieces

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: golden-brown to reddish-brown
  • Sapwood: pale yellow
  • Interlocked grain with natural luster
  • Rich, warm appearance that deepens over time

Cost

  • Moderate to high; hardwood with high density and durability
  • Varies by species and source region

Common Uses

  • Furniture and cabinetry
  • Flooring and decking
  • Decorative paneling and veneers
  • Cutting boards, kitchenware, and turned objects
  • Boat building and outdoor structures

Origin

  • Australia: Acacia melanoxylon (Blackwood)
  • Africa: Various Acacia species used for furniture and flooring
  • Southeast Asia: Plantation-grown species for timber and furniture

Other Characteristics

  • Strong, dense, and resistant to wear
  • High natural oil content for moisture and insect resistance
  • Develops a warm, rich patina over time
  • Stable and suitable for both structural and decorative applications
Acacia wood is a versatile and durable hardwood, valued for furniture, flooring, and decorative woodworking due to its strength, beautiful grain, and natural resistance to decay.
