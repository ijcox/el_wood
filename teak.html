Teak Wood
Teak is a premium tropical hardwood known for its exceptional durability, water resistance, and rich golden-brown color. Ideal for outdoor and high-end furniture.

Teak comes from the Tectona grandis tree, native to South and Southeast Asia. It is widely recognized for its natural oils that protect it from water, insects, and decay.

Return to the Wood overview.

Construction and Workability

  • Medium to hard; moderately easy to work with hand and power tools
  • Finishes well, though natural oils may require special treatment before gluing or staining
  • Ideal for furniture, decking, and boat building
  • Can be planed, sanded, and turned for fine woodworking

Durability

  • Extremely durable; naturally resistant to decay, rot, and insects
  • Excellent for outdoor use, even in humid or marine environments
  • Longevity often exceeds decades without significant maintenance

Pliability

  • Moderately flexible when steamed
  • Used for curved furniture components and boat planking

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: golden to medium brown, darkening with age
  • Sapwood: pale yellow, usually lighter than heartwood
  • Straight grain with occasional interlocked patterns
  • Attractive natural sheen and oily texture

Cost

  • High; tropical hardwood with premium pricing
  • Natural durability reduces long-term maintenance costs

Common Uses

  • Outdoor furniture and garden structures
  • Decking and flooring
  • Boat building and marine applications
  • High-end cabinetry and decorative items
  • Paneling and veneers

Origin

  • Native to India, Indonesia, Myanmar, and Thailand
  • Cultivated in tropical regions worldwide

Other Characteristics

  • High natural oil content provides water and insect resistance
  • Strong, dense, and impact-resistant
  • Resists warping, shrinking, and cracking
  • Develops a silver-gray patina if left untreated outdoors
Teak is a highly prized hardwood for outdoor and luxury applications due to its durability, beauty, and natural resistance to environmental damage.
