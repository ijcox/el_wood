Cedar Wood
Cedar is a softwood known for its aromatic scent, natural resistance to decay, and attractive reddish-brown color.

Cedar comes from trees in the genus Cedrus as well as other species commonly called cedar, such as Western Red Cedar (Thuja plicata) and Eastern Red Cedar (Juniperus virginiana). It is widely used for outdoor furniture, siding, and closets.

Construction and Workability

  • Soft and lightweight; easy to cut and shape
  • Works well with hand and power tools
  • Excellent for joinery, carving, and siding
  • Accepts stains and paints but often left natural for aromatic finish

Durability

  • Highly durable; naturally resistant to decay and insect damage
  • Ideal for outdoor applications and humid environments
  • Can last decades when properly maintained

Pliability

  • Moderately flexible; can be bent with steaming
  • Used for curved panels and outdoor structures

Color and Appearance

  • Heartwood: reddish-brown to deep chestnut
  • Sapwood: pale yellow or cream-colored
  • Fine, straight grain with a soft texture
  • Develops a silver-gray patina if left untreated outdoors

Cost

  • Moderate; Western Red Cedar and Eastern Red Cedar moderately priced
  • Specialty aromatic cedar may be higher in cost

Common Uses

  • Outdoor furniture, decks, and fences
  • Closets, chests, and lining for aroma and moth protection
  • Siding and shingles
  • Boat building and garden structures
  • Decorative panels and carvings

Origin

  • True cedars: Mediterranean regions
  • Western Red Cedar: Pacific Northwest, USA and Canada
  • Eastern Red Cedar: Eastern USA

Other Characteristics

  • Aromatic scent repels insects
  • Lightweight and easy to handle
  • Stable with minimal warping when dried
  • Resistant to moisture and decay
Cedar wood is prized for outdoor and decorative applications due to its natural durability, aromatic properties, and attractive appearance.
